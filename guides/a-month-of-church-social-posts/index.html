Guides / Presence

A month of church social posts, from what already happens

You do not need a content strategy. You need the calendar you already have, and twenty minutes on a Sunday evening.

6 min read · Updated September 24, 2026

Church social media usually fails for one reason: posting depends on someone remembering. The fix is not more creativity. It is deciding once a month instead of deciding every week.

Start with the calendar you already have

Open the church calendar for next month. Every event on it is a post before it happens and a post after it happens. That is most of your month, found in five minutes.

Add the recurring things: the Sabbath School lesson, the sermon, the prayer meeting. You are not inventing content. You are photographing a church that is already busy.

Four kinds of post, rotated

An invitation: something specific, with a date and a place. A moment: a photograph from last Sabbath, with one honest sentence. A thought: one line from the sermon or the lesson, written out, not as a graphic. A person: a member, with permission, saying why they serve.

Rotate those four and you will never face a blank week again.

Decide who takes the photographs

Ask two or three members to take pictures each Sabbath and put them in one shared album. Phone photographs are fine. A shared album solves more of this problem than any scheduling tool.

Ask permission before posting faces, especially children, and keep a note of who has said no.

Twenty minutes on Sunday evening

Once a month, sit down with the calendar and the album, write the posts, and schedule them. Scheduling is the difference between a quiet week and silence.

If the month gets away from you, post fewer times rather than posting nothing. Three consistent posts a month beat twelve in January and none after.

What to do with this

  • Plan a month at a time, not a week at a time.
  • Rotate four kinds of post: invitation, moment, thought, person.
  • Give two or three members a shared album to drop photographs into.
  • Schedule the posts so a busy week does not mean silence.
